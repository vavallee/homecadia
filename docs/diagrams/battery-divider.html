Sensor-01 Battery & Divider

Step 3 puts the sensor on its own cell and gives firmware a way to read the voltage. Three parts and four solder joints, all on the XIAO's underside pads. This is the first step where a mistake destroys hardware rather than just failing to work.

⚠️ Verify battery polarity with a multimeter before the cell touches the board. Reversed polarity destroys the XIAO's charge circuit instantly. Red probe to the lead you believe is +, DC volts, expect +3.5 to +4.2 V. A negative reading means the leads are swapped. Do this on all three cells — do not assume the colours are consistent between them.

Power — the cell is now the supply

LiPo → BAT+/BAT−XIAO underside pads
USB-Ccharges the cell, safe to co-exist
driver board JSTnot used — does not charge

Unlike the bench PSU in step 2, USB and the battery are not mutually exclusive — the XIAO's charge IC expects both. But charging only works with a USB A-to-C cable: the 2-wire panel pigtail has no CC resistors, so a C-to-C supply never enables VBUS.

Not A0. A0 is the ePaper reset line on this build — the Seeed wiki's analogReadMilliVolts(A0) battery snippet is shared XIAO boilerplate and does not apply here. No divider is populated on the board; this one is entirely ours (pinmap.md).

Not the driver board's JST. That path does not charge in this stack — confirmed in veltoc's build; the wiki's charging-IC claim did not hold (assembly.md).

Carrying the three parts

Two options, both in the BOM, decided at test fit:

  • Perfboard offcut — tidier, but the case has no mount for it, so it needs somewhere in the cavity to live.
  • Inline and heat-shrunk — no mount needed, less tidy.

Only three connections genuinely need the XIAO's underside pads: the encoder push switch (MTCK/GPIO6), this divider node (MTDI/GPIO5), and BAT+/BAT−. Everything else solders to the driver board's larger through-holes.

Strapping on GPIO5 — resolved, not a risk

GPIO5 is in Seeed's avoid list, and the permanent divider holds it at ~1.9 V — inside the undefined logic band while straps are sampled, so the strap latches unpredictably.

It does not matter. Per the ESP32-C6 datasheet Table 3-4, MTDI straps the SDIO sampling and driving clock edge and nothing else. sensor-01 uses no SDIO — display is SPI, sensor is I2C, console is native USB. No code path reads the setting, so no gating MOSFET is needed and the 2.1 µA bleed stays as budgeted.

One real cost remains: MTDI is JTAG TDI, so external JTAG through the underside pads is given up.

What success looks like

  • Meter across the cell and the ADC agree: firmware reports Vbat/2 ±5% of what the meter reads at the battery. That closes the HW-VERIFY row in bringup.md.
  • LOW BATT clears from the display once a charged cell is fitted — it appears below LOW_BATTERY_PCT 10%.
  • Matter attribute 3/47/11 (BatVoltage, millivolts) tracks the meter, and 3/47/12 reports a sane percentage.
  • Calibrate the LUT endpoints against the meter at two or three states of charge — the percentage comes from an open-circuit-voltage table, not a coulomb counter, so it is only as good as its endpoints.
Measuring sleep current comes after this, not during. Putting a handheld meter in series browns the C6 out on every Thread poll. The procedure that works with the AstroAI AM33D — bypass jumper, lowest range only once asleep — is field-notes.md section 10. Expect driver board #1's RST leak (~5.5 mA) to dominate anything measured on that board.
