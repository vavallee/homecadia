No-Solder Bring-Up Ladder

Each rung adds one thing and proves it before the next. Everything uses the on-chip connectivity scan, which drives nothing hard — only internal pull resistors and the ADC — so no step can short the board.

Two rules for the whole ladder. Change wiring only with USB unplugged. And never bridge two sockets whose identity you haven't confirmed — that's what browned the board out before, and steps 2 and 3 exist to remove the guessing.
0

Baseline — already passing

Confirms the tester itself is honest before we trust it on anything else.

WiringXIAO alone in the breadboard, nothing else attached.
PassAll six lines FLOATING, no bridges. Confirmed 2026-08-18, along with D2 reading 3285 mV against the XIAO's own 3V3 — so the pin and the voltmeter are both good.
1

Prove each jumper wire, off the board

A bad jumper or a splayed tinned end looks exactly like a wiring mistake. Eliminate the wires as suspects while they're easy to test.

WiringTake one jumper. Put one end in the XIAO's D0 row, the other in the D2 row. Nothing else connected.
PassBRIDGED: RST (D0) <-> BUSY (D2)
FailBoth still FLOATING — the wire or one of its ends is bad. Set it aside and try another.

Repeat for every jumper you plan to use. Say "go" after each and I'll read it out. Tedious once, never again.

2

Find the driver board's ground net

This is the step that finally settles socket identity — measured, not read off a mirrored silkscreen. Ground is the easiest net to find because it's shared across several pins.

WiringXIAO GND → the socket you believe is GND. Then run wires from D0, D1, D2, D3, D8, D10 to six other sockets, noting which socket each one goes to.
ReadAny line reporting held LOW is connected to the board's ground net. Everything else reads FLOATING.

Tell me which socket each wire went to and I'll map it. If nothing reads low, the socket you called GND isn't ground — move that wire one position and repeat. This is safe to iterate: only pull resistors are involved, about 70 microamps.

3

Confirm power reaches the board

Everything downstream is meaningless if the panel has no supply — and this is the measurement we've never actually taken.

Build the safe probeTwo 1 MΩ resistors in series. One free end to the point being measured, the other free end to GND, and the junction between them to D2. That halves the voltage, so any point up to about 6.6 V is safe to touch — the 5 V socket included.
WiringPower the driver board: XIAO 3V3 and GND to the sockets confirmed in step 2. Board switch ON.
ReadThe scan prints both scales. Use the via 1M/1M divider column:
ReadingMeans
~3300 mV3.3 V rail — panel has power
~5000 mVthe 5 V rail — that socket is 5V, not 3V3
~0 mVground
drifting 400–1400floating — nothing there

Probe each socket in turn. By the end you'll have the board mapped by measurement, and the mirror question is dead for good.

4

Prove each signal path end to end

Now that power and ground sockets are known by measurement, bridging two signal sockets is safe, and it tests both jumpers at once.

WiringWith the six signal jumpers in place, add one short wire between two sockets you've confirmed are not power or ground — say RST and CS.
PassBRIDGED: RST (D0) <-> CS (D1) — both jumpers and both socket contacts are good.
FailNo bridge reported — at least one of the two paths is open. Since step 1 cleared the wires, that points at the loose header pin in the hole.

Walk the bridge wire along the pairs until all six are proven. This is where unsoldered pins will most likely bite, and now you'll see exactly which one.

5

Connect the panel

The single check that says the display is really there.

WiringRemove the bridge wire from step 4. Flip the latch up and slide the FPC in — the right orientation goes in with light finger pressure; the reversed one needs force. Push until the stiffener is inside the housing, then close the latch. Power on.
PassBUSY no longer floats — it reports held LOW or held HIGH, because a live panel drives that line. The real proof comes one step later: on a refresh, BUSY must go high for roughly 1.8s (full) or 0.5s (partial). The driver enforces that; a panel that received nothing logs BUSY never rose.
FailBUSY still FLOATING with power confirmed in step 3 and paths confirmed in step 4 → the fault is the FPC seating or its orientation. Power off first, then reseat or flip the ribbon. Reversed, tab n meets panel pin 25−n: VDDIO lands on RST and VCI on BUSY, so driving RST low shorts a supply rail and browns out the board. That killed two panels during bring-up.
Honest expectation

Steps 0–3 should hold steady. Step 4 is where loose pins in unsoldered holes tend to fail, and they may pass once and fail the next time you nudge the bench — that's the nature of the contact, not a mistake on your part. The ladder's real value is that it tells you which connection is open instead of leaving you to infer it from a blank screen. When the iron arrives, soldering those headers converts every intermittent rung into a permanent one.
